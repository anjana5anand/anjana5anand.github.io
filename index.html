Selected work

The model watches 8 steps of a pedestrian's path and samples 16 plausible futures. The tricky part was the noise scale: DDPM expects data around unit scale, but real trajectory displacements are tiny, so the noise was drowning the signal. Fixing that (and switching to proper DDIM sampling) made a big difference in path quality.

Part of ongoing robotics lab research into robot navigation in crowded spaces. The goal is giving a robot a realistic sense of where nearby people might go next.

PyTorch DDPM / DDIM Transformers SiT NeurIPS 2023 MPS · Apple Silicon
GitHub ↗
  • ADE 0.395 m, FDE 0.760 m, best of 16 predictions over 10k trajectory windows
  • 99% of the time at least one predicted path ends within 1 m of where the person actually went
  • Around 5–6 distinct modes per window: predictions spread out at decision points, not just one fan
  • Noise was 14× the data scale; once fixed, training converged much faster
  • Proper DDIM (deterministic) made paths smooth; stochastic DDPM was why they looked jagged
  • Built a little 2D simulator to see predictions in real time alongside simulated pedestrians
  • Evaluated with ADE, FDE, APD, miss rate, coverage, and speed fidelity

16 predicted trajectory fans per pedestrian: observed history (dark), ground-truth future (teal), sampled predictions (warm colours). Warmer = endpoints further from the centroid.
